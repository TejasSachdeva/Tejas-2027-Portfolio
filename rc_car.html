RC Car

Custom Built Remote-Controlled Racing Vehicle

Project Overview

Designed and built a custom RC car from scratch, optimizing the drivetrain, steering geometry, and structural layout for competitive racing. The project spanned mechanical design, DFM, and electronics integration.

Drivetrain Design

The original drivetrain used a gear-based transmission connected to bevel gears at the rear axle. During the race, the bevel gears sheared under load, leaving the car immobilized. With the competition still underway, I redesigned the drivetrain around an HTD synchronous belt and pulley system. The new design was simpler to assemble, easier to align, and more reliable under load. The final system achieved an overall reduction ratio of approximately 10.5:1.

Since all custom parts were FDM printed, DFM was a key consideration throughout. Given FDM's anisotropic layer structure, I added lead-in chamfers on mating features and generous fillets at stress concentrations to reduce the chance of layer delamination under load.

Fit & Assembly

The hub adapter’s press-fit dimensions were refined through several rounds of printed prototypes, adjusting the bore size until the fit was consistent and repeatable across parts without requiring custom measurement tooling.

Steering

Ackermann steering geometry was implemented to minimize wheel scrub during cornering. Using the wheelbase (308 mm) and track width (139 mm), the maximum Ackermann angle worked out to ~47°, giving a theoretical minimum turning radius of 287 mm. The measured value on the final car was 347 mm which was slightly larger; likely due to axle compliance and the geometry of the pre-bought steering assembly we adapted the servo to.
