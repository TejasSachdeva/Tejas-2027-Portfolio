Vision-Based Tactile Sensor

Mechanically Programmable Lattice Sensor for 3-Axis Force Estimation

Project Overview

Designed and built a vision-based tactile sensor that uses SLA-printed diamond lattices to measure contact forces. An embedded camera records lattice deformation, and a compact neural network will use those images to estimate forces along three axes. The lattice geometry can be adjusted in CAD to change stiffness by varying strut diameter, unit cell size, or layer count. This project also investigates whether the printed lattice can withstand repeated loading better than conventional silicone sensing surfaces.

Why Lattices?

Many vision-based tactile sensors, including GelSight, use silicone elastomer casts. These sensors work well, but changing their stiffness generally requires making a new cast, and repeated loading can cause the material to tear. A 3D-printed lattice offers another approach. Its stiffness can be adjusted by changing the strut diameter or unit cell size, then printing the updated design without a new mold or tooling. Printing four specimens takes about five hours. Testing will determine how well the lattice holds up under repeated compression and shear.

Tunable Stiffness
Stiffness can be adjusted in CAD by changing strut diameter, unit cell size, or layer count. No new mold or material is needed.
Fatigue Resistance
The lattice distributes load through its strut network. Repeated compression and shear tests will show whether this design improves durability.
Rapid Reconfiguration
Locating pins align the lattice with the housing, allowing specimens to be changed by hand without removing multiple screws.

Hardware Design

The sensor has four main parts: a top cover that secures the lattice, the lattice specimen, a mid-frame for the LED ring, and a base that holds the camera. The housing was first printed in white PLA, but internal reflections reduced contrast in the camera image. I switched to black PLA to reduce reflected light and make the lattice deformation easier to see.

Locating Pins
Aligns the top and bottom housing halves consistently. The pins also make it easier to swap specimens by hand and reduce setup variation between robot-arm runs.
Lead-in Chamfers
Chamfers around the screw holes help guide fasteners into place and reduce the risk of cracking the PLA, which occurred during early assembly.
External Brim Bracket
The lattice's built-in brim was too thin to attach rigidly to the housing. As a result, the boundary moved with the lattice and added noise to the camera signal. An external bracket now holds the brim in place so the measured deformation comes primarily from the lattice interior.
LED Illumination
An SK6812 LED strip with 144 LEDs/m surrounds the lattice window. Consistent lighting helps limit shadows and bright spots that could appear in the difference image and be mistaken for lattice deformation.

Lattice Specimens

Four diamond-lattice specimens were printed from Formlabs Flexible 50A resin using SLA. The material was selected for its flexibility under low loads. The specimens form a 2×2 matrix that varies strut diameter and the presence of a top plate, allowing their effects on force estimation and durability to be compared.

Vision Pipeline

I replaced an initial marker-tracking approach with image differencing. Each camera frame is compared with a reference image captured at zero load. This captures changes across the lattice without requiring printed markers on its surface. The resulting difference image will be passed to a compact vision model to estimate the applied forces.

Data Collection

Data collection uses a UFACTORY xArm 6 robot arm, a 6-axis force/torque sensor, and a custom 3D-printed probe with a rubber tip. Rubber on the probe and lattice surface is intended to transfer shear into the lattice and reduce slipping. The planned test includes 2,400 loading cycles across normal and shear conditions. Force measurements from the sensor will be paired with the corresponding camera frames.

Normal Loading
1,200 cycles from 0–20 N to evaluate force-prediction accuracy and consistency across repeated loading. Changes in the response over time may indicate fatigue.
Shear Loading
1,200 cycles with 0–5 N of lateral force applied under a normal preload. Rubber-on-rubber contact is intended to transfer shear while limiting slip.
Ground Truth
The xArm's 6-axis force/torque sensor provides force labels for each camera frame. An RSA-G2 Dynamic Mechanical Analyzer will be used for additional material characterization.
